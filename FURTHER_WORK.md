# Further work: from the moderate review to a full study

## Current boundary

The project has a moderate targeted literature review, including a completed matched-outcome corrective search; it is not a systematic review. The [common matrix](research/09_balanced_literature_review.md) records verified additions and remaining gaps. The completed search is recorded in [the query and decision log](sources/moderate_review_search_2026.md). Additional searching alone does not establish complete coverage or make incomparable outcomes comparable. The steps below are proposed work, not tasks already completed.

## 1. Define and register the review protocol

Specify populations, internet-distributed media content, eligible designs, comparators, outcomes, languages and dates before the full search. Narrow the main questions enough to answer them. Register or timestamp the protocol and keep an amendment log. Distinguish a review of causal effects from a broader map of mechanisms and associations.

Predefine three separate exposure comparisons: equal attentive time; complete works; and repeated practices over a common period. Code creator relationships and community participation separately from content exposure. Define direct listening and embedded music, mixed feeds, digital books, video purposes and genres, and cross-format overlap. Do not silently substitute one exposure unit for another.

Specify immediate experience, belief/attitude change, intentions, observed completed action, persistent behavior, and major life decisions as different outcomes. Predefine follow-up windows and what counts as a consequential action. Include beneficial, harmful, null and uncertain results.

**Deliverable:** timestamped protocol, eligibility rules, exposure taxonomy and outcome dictionary.

## 2. Conduct and archive a comprehensive search

Develop and pilot database-specific strings with a research librarian or information specialist where available. Search relevant psychology, communication, education and multidisciplinary databases; candidate sources include PsycINFO, Communication & Mass Media Complete, Scopus or Web of Science, and PubMed where relevant. Add suitable preprint, dissertation and trial-registration sources. Record unavailable databases and language restrictions rather than implying access.

Use a documented category-by-outcome search matrix across all eight categories. Include synonyms, historical terminology, controlled vocabulary where supported, and studies reporting no effect. Check references and forward citations of eligible syntheses and key studies. Update searches shortly before release. Export exact strings, platform names, dates, result counts and complete records; retain stable identifiers and deduplication decisions.

**Deliverable:** reproducible search appendix, archived exports, deduplicated library and update log. Neither the initial seven retained queries nor the new 32 matched queries and 18 retrieval/gap searches supply a complete database export or independent screening record.

## 3. Screen and retrieve with independent checks

Have two reviewers independently screen titles/abstracts and full texts, pilot the rules, resolve disagreements and document adjudication. Record one explicit reason for each full-text exclusion. Seek accessible manuscripts or author copies for blocked sources; mark unresolved retrievals. Maintain a flow of identified, deduplicated, screened, excluded and included records without inventing historical counts.

**Deliverable:** screening decisions, full-text exclusion register, unresolved-access list and review flow diagram.

## 4. Extract and appraise the evidence

Pilot an extraction form and independently verify key data: samples, recruitment, randomization or identification strategy, exposure/dose, comparator, outcome instrument, follow-up, attrition, effects and uncertainty, preregistration, funding and conflicts. Assess risk of bias with tools appropriate to each design; document judgments and reasons. Audit overlap between reviews and primary studies to avoid double counting.

Assess directness to the project's population, media content, online distribution and exposure unit. An abstract-only candidate remains provisional until the required fields are checked. Record unavailable details explicitly.

**Deliverable:** checked study-level extraction table, risk-of-bias assessments and synthesis-overlap map.

## 5. Synthesize only compatible comparisons

Group evidence by exposure unit, outcome, follow-up and study design before considering aggregation. Use structured narrative synthesis where pooling is inappropriate. Meta-analyze only substantively compatible contrasts, accounting for multiple outcomes and dependent samples. Assess heterogeneity, small-study/reporting concerns and sensitivity to study quality when evidence permits. Do not rank media by comparing effect sizes against unrelated control groups.

For each conclusion, report the supporting body of evidence, directness, risk of bias, consistency, precision and missing-evidence concerns. Use an explicit certainty framework appropriate to the design and question; do not present the current informal labels as a completed standardized certainty assessment. Report search coverage separately.

**Deliverable:** outcome-specific synthesis with traceable conclusions and reasons for certainty judgments. Missing evidence and non-significant findings remain distinct from evidence supporting a practically negligible effect.

## 6. Establish what the literature still cannot answer

A full review may still find no fair comparison of life influence across formats. Report that result without filling it with radar judgments. If the comparative question remains central, design new research: the [proposed panel and exposure study](results/03_next_study.md) provides a starting point. Add power calculations, recruitment/attrition plans, preregistered analyses and ethical review before data collection. Randomization or justified quasi-experimental identification is needed for causal claims; a longitudinal association alone is insufficient.

**Deliverable:** either a qualified literature-based answer or a clearly bounded gap and a separate primary-study protocol. A systematic review and a new causal study are different completion milestones.

## Completion criteria

Call the review systematic only after the protocol, archived searches, independent screening, checked extraction, design-appropriate bias appraisal and transparent synthesis are complete and published with the report. Call the project a causal cross-medium comparison only when exposure, outcomes, populations and identification support that claim. Neither milestone requires declaring a winning medium.
