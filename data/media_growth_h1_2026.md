## Measurement and interpretation

Chart footnote: ages **16–64**; respondents selected days of engagement in the past week and the duration of the latest session. GWI segmented respondents by total weekly usage and used each segment's midpoint to calculate the weekly average. These are self-reported, grouped estimates, not instrumented screen-time records. The release describes 49 countries; exact country weights and matched-wave sample composition have not been audited. The broader release allows 16+ in selected markets, whereas this chart explicitly specifies 16–64; retain the chart's narrower population here.

Short video has the highest reported percentage increase among these 12 categories, **+5.2%**, followed by mobile gaming at +4.2%: a **1.0 percentage-point** gap, without published uncertainty in the screenshot. This is a ranking of point estimates, not a tested difference. Short video is below social media on current weekly time (7:06 versus 7:07).

The implied increase computed from rounded entries is 426 − 426 / 1.052 = about **21.1 minutes**, consistent with the release's +21 minutes. This check is arithmetic, not an independently measured baseline or replication. It does not validate the older project's +24-minute cross-release difference.

## Correspondence to the project's eight formats

Short and long video are close proxies, without isolated vertical/horizontal orientation. Music streaming excludes the union of embedded and standalone music. Games are split by device and their audiences/time must not be assumed disjoint. Video streaming (example: Netflix) does not separate films, series and other programmes. Digital books and standalone pictures have no separate row. Print and online press cannot stand in for digital books. Social media is a mixed-content activity. Categories and concurrent consumption must not be treated as an exclusive attention budget.

Confidence is **moderate** in the bounded descriptive growth ranking and in the short-video versus long-video/streaming comparison. All-eight-format leadership and a vertical-versus-horizontal comparison remain unresolved. The chart provides no life-impact result.
