# Social breadth of short-video consumption

Assessment date: **9 November 2026**. Addresses H27 in [Project hypotheses](06_hypotheses.md).

## Assessment

**Partial negative answer:** the evidence argues against treating short video as generally more evenly consumed across social groups than other media. A same-survey platform comparison supplies counterexamples to that broad claim, without establishing a universal ordering of creative media.

**Confidence:** moderate for the bounded platform comparison; low for generalization to short-video consumption across the project's media categories. Viewing time, the target format, and most non-platform comparison media are not isolated.

## Comparable platform evidence: PEWSOC26

Pew's 2026 US table reports platform use within household-income groups:

| Platform | Under $30,000 (%) | $100,000+ (%) | Absolute endpoint gap (percentage points) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | 44 | 29 | 15 |
| YouTube | 77 | 90 | 13 |
| Facebook | 69 | 70 | 1 |
| Instagram | 42 | 59 | 17 |

TikTok's educational endpoints are 43% for high school or less versus 28% for college graduates. These are within-group adoption rates, not audience composition. [Original tables](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/#who-uses-each-social-media-platform).

Gaps are this project's subtraction of rounded point estimates. TikTok is less even than some comparators and more even than another on this endpoint metric. Differences between gap estimates were not tested for significance; no precise inequality ranking is claimed.

## Measurement limits

The Pew question asks whether people ever use a platform, including rare use. It does not measure weekly viewing, minutes, or Reels/Shorts specifically. [Original questionnaire](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/10/PI_2026.10.19_NPORS-Internet-Fact-Sheets_Questionnaire.pdf).

The address-based national survey included 5,511 US adults, January 30–June 18, 2026, using web, paper, and phone responses. Overall margin of error is ±1.8 percentage points; subgroup margins are larger. Overall precision is not the uncertainty of a difference between gaps. [Original methodology](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/10/PI_2026.10.19_NPORS-Internet-Fact-Sheets_Methodology.pdf).

Facebook, YouTube, and Instagram also distribute short video; TikTok contains noncreative content. These platforms are not disjoint media formats. Higher adoption in a lower-income group does not establish equality: reversing a gradient differs from reducing its magnitude. Cheap access is a possible explanation, not a cause identified here. Age composition and preferences could contribute.

## Additional original research

**OFCOM25:** the UK report, based on 2024 fieldwork, finds no home internet in 15% of DE households versus 2% of AB households. This is digital-access context, not a short-video consumption rate. Social grade is not household income or area deprivation. [Original report, printed page 4](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/adults/adults-media-use-and-attitudes-2025/adults-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025.pdf?v=396240).

**CFPSSHORT25:** *Digital gratification: short video consumption and mental health in rural China* uses CFPS and defines frequent use as watching short videos almost every day. Frequent users in its selected rural sample have more schooling than non-frequent users. This describes selected groups, not age-adjusted education-specific adoption rates or a comparison with other media. It cautions against transporting a social gradient across populations. Health-effect estimates are not used for H27. [Original methods and Table 1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1536191/full).

The existing [England arts-participation aggregate](../data/participation_and_breadth.md) is context, not short-video evidence. Its 81%/95% contrast must not be compared numerically with US TikTok gaps. Australian creation evidence also measures a different activity.

## Requirements for a full test

Use one population and period for short video and each comparator, with participation rates and all-person consumption time in the same income and education groups. Include nonusers and offline adults. Report both raw and age-standardized socioeconomic gaps.

Predefine evenness: absolute percentage-point gaps, participation-rate ratios, or a distribution-wide measure. These can disagree when overall adoption differs. Compare all groups and estimate uncertainty for differences in inequality. Apply a common content definition. No harmonized cross-medium comparison was obtained in this targeted search; no global equality score or radar recalibration is produced.
