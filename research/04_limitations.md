# Limitations

## Literature search coverage and confidence

The literature review is exploratory, with a moderate documented targeted update on 9 November 2026. The [log](../sources/moderate_review_search_2026.md) records eight web queries and selected candidate decisions. It is not a systematic database search: English-language discovery, search-engine ranking, selective follow-up and variable full-text access can omit relevant positive, null or conflicting studies. Earlier searches lack a complete retrospective screening record. There was no independent second screener, exhaustive citation chasing, complete hit export or formal study-level risk-of-bias assessment.

Coverage and appraisal depth differ across categories. Several new candidates are abstract-only; long-video creator studies do not verify the duration/orientation or artistic-content criteria. Outcomes range from cognition and intentions to observed behavior and delayed responses. This evidence cannot support a cross-medium life-impact ranking. “Not found in the documented search” is not evidence that a study does not exist, and missing evidence is not evidence of no effect. Non-significant findings also do not by themselves establish a practically negligible effect.

Confidence labels describe a specified conclusion using available design quality, directness, consistency and precision. They are informal narrative assessments, not calibrated probabilities or a completed standardized certainty appraisal. Search completeness is a separate limitation and cannot be repaired by raising or lowering a confidence label. The [further-work plan](../FURTHER_WORK.md) sets out the protocol, database search, independent screening, bias appraisal and synthesis needed for a full review.

## Overlapping art forms

Music is present in short vertical and long horizontal videos, online films, series and games. This is a substantive part of online musical exposure, not measurement noise. A minute of a music-driven video can be both video exposure and music exposure. Counting both is legitimate for separate artistic-contact questions; adding them into a person's exclusive time budget double-counts the minute. Books also circulate through adaptations. The same intellectual property can generate multiple revenues and audiences.

## Medium is not art

Platform and television totals contain non-artistic material. A vertical orientation, an algorithmic feed, or a streaming subscription does not identify artistic content. Conversely, artistic work can circulate outside the platform most associated with it.

## Additional limitations introduced by long horizontal video

- **Proxy mismatch:** the new art category requires duration, landscape orientation, and creative treatment. GWI's long-online-video aggregate does not isolate those properties. Its reach, time, and frequency must retain the proxy label; even the fraction qualifying as art is unknown.
- **Arbitrary boundaries:** 10 minutes is an operational proposal. Landscape videos can be short; long videos can be vertical. Documentary films, episodic vlogs, video podcasts and music performances require primary-category rules and secondary tags. Classification needs independent coders and agreement checks before population estimates.
- **More overlap:** a viewer can encounter the same creator or work in a Short, a full video, a podcast, and a film. Shorts may direct people to longer works. Audience shares and time cannot be added without person-level de-duplication; influence cannot be attributed to length from these aggregates.
- **Mixed mechanisms:** essays, vlogs, performances, explainers, and interviews vary in narrative, factual information, and creator relationships. A useful tutorial's effect on a career is not automatically an artistic effect. The existing narrative-persuasion literature does not validate an effect estimate for this new category.
- **Dose and creator confounding:** a longer work has more exposure time, while repeated contact with a creator or community may explain significance. Duration, genre, prior interest, attention, and creator attachment need separate measurement. Autoplay, skipped segments, playback speed, and background listening further separate reported minutes from attentive exposure.
- **Financial and historical gaps:** no isolated 2021–2025 revenue or matched consumption series is selected for the new category. Platform totals mix formats and revenue streams; sponsorship, memberships, and off-platform income can overlap. Adding the category increases missing coverage rather than completing the market comparison.
- **Additional subjective judgments:** the extension originally added ten radar values and rationales. After removal of detailed personal-impact scores, the current package retains 48 subjective numeric scores, including eight general Life impact judgments. Three consumption anchors are broader proxies and the money rating is qualitative. The apparent position of long video relative to books, films, or short video is an author hypothesis, not new empirical evidence about life change.

## Online-distribution scope

The target is internet-distributed creative content. Radar consumption and money judgments were revised on 9 November 2026; broad listening, print-inclusive books, cinema receipts and US time diaries no longer anchor them. Contextual tables are retained with their original boundaries. Missing online-only measurements remain missing. The inclusive music profile is a qualitative scenario covering direct and embedded audible contact, with a separate streaming-only reference. Its reach, time and frequency have hollow markers because no measured union is available. Neither its exact uplift nor its ordering against short video is identified. Film, digital books, pictures and online-only revenues remain particularly uncertain; lowering an author rating does not measure the digital share of a broader market.

## Denominators and geographic coverage

The empirical materials mix international online-adult surveys, US population diaries, global industry estimates. No single target population is represented by all eight categories. Children, offline audiences, and countries excluded from particular surveys can change the ordering.

## Incomplete and changing time series

Five complete years means 2021–2025. The 2026 survey is a separate partial-year snapshot. Historical survey redesigns, revised market estimates, exchange rates, pandemic closures prevent simple trend comparisons. Missing observations remain gaps. No yearly series of life-changing influence is fabricated.

## Limits of the updated growth evidence

The [additional growth appraisal](08_growth_evidence_search.md) supports moderate confidence in service growth within several measured audiences. Ofcom/Ipsos iris covers UK online adults and whole services; Qustodio covers monitored children and app use. Neither directly compares artistic short vertical video with all other media. GMI26 reports leading growth for short video, but shares the GWI survey family with DataReportal. Its public release does not resolve paired waves, common country/age coverage, aggregate weights, uncertainty, or absolute-versus-percentage ranking. H04 and H05 therefore remain low-confidence tentative answers. The older +5.97% and +0.67% are published-value differences, establishing neither consumption direction nor relative growth. CNNIC/QuestMobile and Sensor Tower leads remain unaudited and do not increase confidence.

## Selection, attribution, and causality

People may select books or videos because they are already considering a decision. A work can be a catalyst, a justification, a source of language, or one influence among many. Retrospective statements do not isolate its causal contribution. An intervention's effect on a narrow behavior cannot be assumed to generalize to migration, career changes, or relationships.

## Event-centered outcomes and cumulative change

The earlier central framing emphasized conspicuous moves, jobs and relationships. This could systematically undercount ordinary changes in mood, self-esteem, habits, social belonging and perceived norms, especially for music, images and feeds. The [measurement definition](01_scope_and_metrics.md#what-life-changing-would-require) now requires separate event and everyday/cumulative outputs, and the [new review](08_cumulative_media_effects.md) adds relevant evidence. A lack of major events is not evidence of no everyday impact.

The correction does not close the evidence gap. Repeated state reactions are not automatically persistent changes; a long observation period is not itself evidence of accumulation. Temporal aggregation, reverse selection, concurrent offline experiences and person-specific differences can change associations. Null average trajectories and unsuccessful regulation must remain visible. Much of the new online evidence measures entire platforms or messaging rather than artistic content; selected music data do not isolate streaming or embedded soundtracks. The norm-change example uses offline radio and remains contextual only. Evidence is insufficient for a calibrated eight-medium comparison of cumulative life influence.

## Contact versus cumulative environment

One book versus one 30-second clip compares different doses. A year of reading versus a year of using a feed asks a different question. Repetition may make individually minor contacts consequential. This dossier does not estimate exposure-response functions or cumulative causal effects. The new review distinguishes observed repeated-measure patterns from causal accumulation and from persistence after exposure; it includes null and mixed findings.

## Background exposure versus attention

Listening can occur while working or traveling. Watching a film can involve concentrated attention. These experiences differ in attention without one being inherently more valuable. No validated attention-weighting factor is applied.

## What the radar scores are

All 48 numeric radar values are subjective judgments. Life impact is subjective author interpretation of potential lasting influence on an engaged person. Research supporting these exact scores or their comparative ordering has not been found in the project review. These are fresh judgments requested by the user, not averages of the former detailed axes. A numeric anchor informs a judgment but is not a mathematical conversion. Broad-format judgments are sometimes informed by narrower proxies. The score rationale explicitly identifies those proxies.

The model shows aggregate reach, consumption, market scale, and cultural recognition; general life impact reflects author interpretation of potential influence on an engaged person. It is a profile, not a latent scientific index. The suggested ±2-point latitude is a qualitative caution, not a confidence interval, expert-consensus range, or computed uncertainty estimate.

## Bias in the impact ratings

The former detailed impact scores implied an unsupported comparative advantage, particularly for books over short video. Those axes and scores have been removed. Their replacement, general Life impact, now has author scores at the user's request. There is no calibrated evidence-to-score procedure or matched comparison. The higher book score is an author assumption and may reflect bias. No former impact scores were averaged or transferred. Genres and individual works vary greatly within each category.

## Radar geometry

Polygon area depends on axis order and on an arbitrary ordinal scale. Adjacent dimensions are correlated. The current profiles are closed and lightly filled and are displayed in alphabetical category order. Their filled areas do not establish overall superiority. No area ranking or sum is computed. The axis order and scale are held constant only to make profile differences readable.

## Limits of the participation extension

The two added official survey programmes measure different participation concepts, populations, and periods. Broad arts aggregates cannot establish social breadth for each of the eight categories. Group sample sizes are smaller than total samples, weighting affects precision, and nonresponse and recall remain concerns. Repeated cross-sectional surveys do not track individual audience retention. The new tables are not comparable 2026 global observations and do not validate the subjective radar scores. See the [survey notes](../data/participation_and_breadth.md).

## Evidence and retrieval limits

This is a targeted review without preregistration or exhaustive screening. Some material was available as a publisher abstract, indexed search extract, or a third-party mirror of an original report. Public pages may change. No full source archive, survey microdata, independent re-analysis, or peer review is included. Retrieval status is documented in the source register.

**Figure reproducibility is stronger than source-data reproducibility.** The scripts deterministically regenerate visualizations from the transcribed Markdown tables, but this does not reproduce or independently verify the extraction of those numbers from the original sources. Some evidence is retained only through mutable web pages, search extracts, or materials without archived copies. Available original tables, exact supporting excerpts, and source-version dates have not been systematically preserved. A retrieval date alone does not identify the exact content or version consulted. Consequently, a reader can reproduce the visualization of the transcribed values more confidently than repeat their extraction and check their original context. The [source register](../sources/source_register.md) records access and use, but is not a complete versioned source archive.

## Pictures-specific limitations

Photography, illustration, image memes, and digital reproductions have different purposes and barriers. The category is a working umbrella. Low technical entry barriers for a phone photograph do not establish equal access to professional creation or a sense that an art form is “for people like me.” No common belonging measure was selected.

Attention, machine-predicted memorability, recall after sharing, curated-gallery well-being, and major life changes are distinct outcomes. A one-image exposure, a carousel, and months in a mixed feed are different doses. AI-assisted production and reposting need separate coding. The all-hollow pictures radar contains ten uncertain author judgments; none of its consumption or revenue values has a matched numerical anchor. The later online-scope revision reviews all eight profiles; pictures retain their existing online-only judgments. No measured superiority of pictures over another category is claimed.
