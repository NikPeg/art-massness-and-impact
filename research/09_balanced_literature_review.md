# Moderate literature review: matched outcomes across eight formats

The [corrective search](../sources/balanced_review_search_2026.md) applies the same three outcome blocks to all eight formats: affect/well-being/self-regulation (A); beliefs/attitudes/social cognition (B); and behavior with follow-up extraction (C). It adds 32 matched queries and 18 paper-retrieval/gap searches. This corrects the earlier allocation of different outcomes to different media. It does not make the selected experiments a matched eight-format experiment.

## Coverage and permitted comparisons

**D** means relevant online exposure was verified in the available methods; **P** means provisional abstract-level evidence; **X** means a distribution/exposure mismatch and context only; **—** means no eligible appraised addition for that cell in this update, not no studies or no effect. Multiple labels can refer to one study, not independent evidence. Earlier retained sources are marked “existing”. A positive finding is never implied by D/P.

The matrix distinguishes task performance, feelings, self-reported functioning, intentions and completed actions. No percentage of “positive studies”, pooled cross-format effect, or league table is calculated. Search counts are effort counts, not evidence strength. Categories share samples when cross-tagged; short digital serial drama must not count as two independent replications.

## New appraisals: direct or provisional online evidence

### Short humorous videos: HUMOR23

Stieger et al. (2023) followed 57 people watching preselected humorous clips daily for three weeks, with repeated ratings over the following half hour. The institutional abstract reports immediate stress/well-being benefits that faded and no consistent three-week improvement. This is provisional: publisher access failed, randomization/control details remain unchecked. A curated humorous clip is not an algorithmic TikTok feed; a three-week intervention is not evidence of three-week persistence after exposure stops. [Institutional record](https://kris.kl.ac.at/en/publications/short-medium-and-long-term-impact-of-watching-humorous-video-clip/).

### One digital-series episode: SHORTDRAMA23

Lewis-Smith et al. (2023) randomized 686 girls aged 13–18 to one targeted drama episode or a control video. The original abstract reports no body-satisfaction difference, improved appearance-diversity acceptance, and reduced appearance-related internalized racism among Black girls. Only the indexed abstract was accessible; timing of delayed measurement and full bias assessment remain unverified. This is one episode, cross-tagged as series/short video, with no inference about a whole season or ordinary viewing. Author consulting relationships are disclosed in the source record. [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37659248/).

### Music and cognitive appraisal: MUSCOG23

Smith et al. (2023) report three randomized song experiments (336, 302 and 323 US adults). The first did not support its hypotheses; the latter two found more favorable affect/event-specific appraisals with familiar upbeat music. Methods/results were inspected in the author-hosted published paper. The manipulated song, familiarity, choice and comparison condition matter; immediate appraisal is not durable worldview change or completed action. No post-exposure persistence conclusion is drawn. [Published paper](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~ghaeffel/download/smith-et-al.-2023.pdf).

### Online feature film: FILM24

Reddan et al. (2024) compared *Just Mercy* with control films online (749 participants). Empathic accuracy and reform attitudes improved. Mock-petition responses were not significant (signing p=.07; sharing p=.06) and do not verify completed civic action. Crucially, this article analyzes the first two visits within three days, although the parent trial spans three months; it supplies no three-month persistence result. Relevant full-text methods/results were inspected. This is a content contrast between films, not film versus short video. [Publisher full text](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2322819121).

### Online short fiction and mood: BOOKMOOD22

Carney and Robertson (2022), Studies 2 and 3, compare digitally presented stories with factual control texts. Inspected results show no significant between-group improvements on the reported distress/mood outcomes from direct reading. Study 1's uncontrolled recall and later social-media discussion analyses cannot rescue those causal contrasts. The six-day interval precedes reading and must not be described as six-day post-reading persistence. Stories are not complete novels; copyright-age and self-selection constraints limit generalization. [Original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8993009/).

### Electronic fiction over four weeks: READRCT23

Dodell-Feder et al. (online 2023) randomized 210 adults to fiction, nonfiction or no pleasure reading, with electronic materials for 45 minutes/day, five days/week over four weeks. Full-text methods and results were inspected. Fiction showed no advantage on theory of mind, empathy or social functioning. These are not completed major life decisions. Social content differs between reading arms, other media use is uncontrolled, and the trial does not establish an enduring post-treatment null. [Author-hosted published article](https://psnlab.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf641/files/documents/Dodell-Feder_2023.pdf).

## Revised conclusions

The former presentation could make reading look mainly cognitively beneficial, music/games mainly prosocial and pictures mainly therapeutic simply because those outcomes were searched. That impression is withdrawn. Same-outcome appraisal now includes digital-reading nulls, music appraisal beyond prosociality, short-video affect beyond persuasion, and an online film attitude experiment with a non-significant action proxy.

There is still no matched comparison of all eight online formats on the same population, outcome, dose and follow-up. Long horizontal creator video remains the largest directly applicable causal gap in the appraised material; finding creator-content analyses does not close it. Some feature-film and picture cells also remain empty. This update improves coverage and exposes those gaps rather than manufacturing equal evidence counts. It cannot establish that one medium causes more lasting life change.

“Moderate targeted review” names the work performed; it does not certify comprehensive search coverage or moderate confidence in every conclusion. The remaining limits are documented in [limitations](04_limitations.md#literature-search-coverage-and-confidence). The subjectively scored radars are unchanged by this literature update.
