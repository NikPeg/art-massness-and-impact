# Scope and measurement

## Units of comparison

The target is creative content distributed through the internet: short video, long horizontal creator video, music, feature films, series, books, video games, and pictures. Music means online musical exposure, including direct listening and audible music embedded in short vertical video, long horizontal video, online films, series and games; films and series mean online viewing; books mean digitally distributed reading; games may be downloaded or streamed and need not require an always-online connection. Pictures are defined below.

For every work, record a primary format and secondary artistic components (including music, images and narrative). A viewer can therefore have both video exposure and music exposure in the same minute. These are overlapping contact measures, not exclusive shares of attention or independently attributable effects. Music embedded in audiovisual works is part of the research target; its unmeasured union with direct listening must not be replaced by either streaming-only reach or summed platform audiences.

Existing whole-market revenues, cinema box office, print-inclusive publishing, general listening, population time diaries, and broad arts-participation surveys are retained only as contextual evidence. They do not measure the target online market or its audience directly. Missing online-only observations remain gaps; the scope change creates no new measurements.

A medium, a delivery format, a market, and an art form are different things. Reels is a branded product; short-form vertical video is a format. Music and literature are practices whose works circulate through multiple formats. News, advertising, tutorials, sport, and private recordings occur inside the media-use aggregates.

The dossier uses eight practical categories rather than claiming that they are mutually exclusive artistic species. Other relevant forms include comics, manga, webtoons, stand-up, audio drama, and artistic podcasts. Animation can occur in films, series, games, and short video.

## Pictures

The category includes photography, illustration, drawings, digitized paintings, collages, and image memes distributed online as individual images, image posts, or carousels. These are working inclusion rules, not a claim that all photographs or memes are art. Include authored expressive or aesthetic treatment; tag documentary, humorous, personal, and AI-assisted work separately. Exclude animated GIFs, video slideshows, utilitarian screenshots, pure advertising, and long sequential comics/webtoons treated as separate works. A painting is included through its online image, not physical exhibition attendance or sale.

Record consumption, creation, remixing, and redistribution separately. A repost is not necessarily original creation; an image impression is not attentive viewing. Embedded text is allowed in memes but should be coded to distinguish visual and textual mechanisms. Count a carousel as a post and record its number of images separately. Screen time in a mixed feed cannot be assigned entirely to pictures.

No matched picture-only reach, person-time, frequency, or revenue observation was selected for the common 2026 snapshot or 2021–2025 financial series. They remain NA. The [participation evidence](../data/participation_and_breadth.md#pictures-and-digital-creation-australia-report-2026) and [research appraisals](02_literature_review.md#pictures-research-2024-2026) use different populations and outcomes.

## Long-form horizontal online video

The long-video category is creator-led horizontal online video, such as video essays, authored vlogs, edited entertainment, creative explainers, and recorded performances made for online distribution. It is distinct from feature films and scripted series even when those works are uploaded to YouTube. YouTube is an example distribution platform, not the category itself.

For a future coded sample, use an explicit working rule: landscape aspect ratio (width greater than height), at least 10 minutes, and an authored creative or expressive treatment. The 10-minute cutoff is a study convention, not an industry definition or a quality threshold. Test alternatives above 3 minutes and at 20 minutes. Exclude feature films, scripted-series episodes, raw livestreams, unedited gameplay, pure news bulletins, utilitarian tutorials, and advertising; include an essay about games or a creatively authored documentary vlog under its primary work type. Borderline documentary films require a recorded coding decision; upload destination alone cannot resolve them. Record secondary tags for music, games, and other overlaps rather than summing their time twice.

GWI's **long online video** is only a consumption proxy: it does not establish this duration cutoff, horizontal orientation, creative status, or these exclusions. Its statistics therefore describe the broader source category, not a measured audience for this newly defined art category. Platform categories are different again: YouTube can classify a wide-format video as long-form without requiring 10 minutes. [YouTube format guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en).

## Research dimensions

Life influence is studied through two separately reported outcome families: major events and everyday/cumulative changes. These distinguish possible research outcomes, not current radar axes. The radars retain five scored axes and a general Life impact axis with no numerical assessment; the detailed personal-impact ratings have been removed.

| Dimension | Preferred measurement | Principal qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | Unique consumers within a fixed week or month | Accounts, memberships, visits, and starts are not unique people |
| Time | Person-hours in a fixed population and period | Concurrent consumption requires de-duplication |
| Frequency | Days used per week | Days are not sessions or content items |
| Money | Revenue under an explicit industry boundary | Platform revenue, trade income, and ticket gross differ |
| Cultural recognition | Recognition of specified works after a stated interval | Catalog age and adaptation complicate comparisons |
| Personal significance | Validated meaningfulness or appreciation measures | A strong experience is not demonstrated life change |
| Everyday affect | Repeated mood level, variability and recovery measures during ordinary use | A repeated state response is not automatically a lasting baseline shift |
| Self-evaluation | Separate repeated state self-esteem/body-evaluation measures from trait measures | Body satisfaction is not a complete measure of self-worth |
| Habits | Repeated observed behavior and validated automaticity measures | Frequent use and intentions alone do not establish a habit |
| Belonging | Repeated connection, loneliness and group-identification measures, kept distinct | Friendship closeness is not identical to cultural belonging |
| Perceived norms | Repeated estimates of what others do and approve, alongside personal endorsement | Descriptive norms, injunctive norms and one's own beliefs are different |
| Belief change | Baseline-to-follow-up change against a comparison group | Direction, magnitude, and durability should be reported |
| Action | Observed, predefined consequential behavior | Intention, clicking, and completed action must remain distinct |
| Persistence | Retained effect at later follow-ups | Retrospective memory is a different outcome |
| Biography | A work's reported role in identity or a turning point | Attribution is subjective and vulnerable to recall bias |

Total person-hours equal population size multiplied by average time across that population. Alternatively, use audience size multiplied by time per audience member. Do not multiply an all-population average by the participation share again. Daily average and annual total imply the same ranking only when denominators and periods match.

## Additional dimensions of mass participation

- **Social breadth:** participation rates within age, education, income or area-deprivation groups. A broad total audience can coexist with unequal participation. Area deprivation must not be substituted for individual income.
- **Territorial breadth:** participation rates within defined regions, using a common questionnaire and population. A country's inclusion in a survey does not by itself show widespread participation there.
- **Active artistic participation:** the population share creating or performing art over a fixed period, distinct from consuming it. Overlapping activities require a measured union, not addition of percentages.

Selected observations from England and the EU are recorded in the [participation tables](../data/participation_and_breadth.md). They supplement the research-dimension framework; no composite score or additional radar axis is introduced.

## Why view counts are unsuitable

Different durations produce different counts for equal time. One hour contains 120 complete 30-second clips, or half of a two-hour film. The resulting 240-to-one count ratio is arithmetic, not evidence that the clips have 240 times the impact.

YouTube changed Shorts views on 31 March 2025 to count starts and replays without a minimum watch duration. Its public statement of over 200 billion daily Shorts views cannot be converted into completed works, unique people, or artistic influence. [YouTube counting definition](https://developers.google.com/youtube/reporting/revision_history?hl=en); [platform statistics](https://blog.youtube/press/).

## What “life-changing” would require

Life influence includes major events and changes in everyday experience. A biographical turning point is one possible outcome, not the admission criterion for influence. Report the following two families separately, without converting one into the other or an overall score.

| Outcome family | What to record | Required distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major events | Completed move, job change, relationship decision, or another predefined event; occurrence, timing and contribution attributed to the work | Intention and retrospective attribution are not completed action or causal proof |
| Everyday and cumulative changes | Mood and its variability, self-esteem, habits, perceived belonging, and perceived descriptive/injunctive norms; repeated measures of level, trajectory, duration and personal importance | Immediate response, repeated response, baseline shift and post-exposure persistence are different results |

A recurring small change in mood or belonging can matter without a major event. It can be beneficial, harmful, mixed or null; event magnitude does not determine value. Record state self-esteem separately from stable self-evaluation, friendship closeness separately from cultural belonging, and beliefs about what others do/approve separately from personal endorsement. Report unsuccessful regulation and opposing individual trajectories as well as favorable averages.

Use baseline plus repeated diary or experience-sampling measurements to assess everyday influence. Preregister time windows, outcomes and meaningful-change criteria; use comparable validated measures where available. Estimate within-person deviations and longer-term changes separately from differences between people. Repeated short-lived responses do not establish a lasting baseline shift, while failure to find persistence does not erase a recurrent effect during use. Include follow-up after exposure changes or ends when making a persistence claim.

The [cumulative-effects review](08_cumulative_media_effects.md) and [separate results](../results/06_event_and_cumulative_outcomes.md) distinguish direct online evidence, broader platform evidence and out-of-scope mechanisms. For mixed audiovisual works, record audible embedded music and other components; the presence of music is substantive, while causal component attribution requires a separate design.

For an event outcome, reach multiplied by the probability of a defined attributable event is one proposed population calculation. It does not measure total life impact or replace everyday trajectories. For everyday outcomes, report population coverage and outcome-specific repeated-measure effects over a stated interval; do not sum correlated states, invent a common unit across scales, or treat the absence of an event as zero influence. Neither calculation is estimated as a cross-art index here.
