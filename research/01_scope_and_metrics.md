# Scope and measurement

## Units of comparison

The project compares media formats distributed through the internet: short online video, long online video, music, feature films, series, digital books, video games and pictures. Content may entertain, inform, instruct, advertise or document everyday life. Eligibility does not depend on aesthetic quality, authorial expression or purpose. These are practical, overlapping categories rather than a complete or exclusive taxonomy.

### Comparison A: primary work formats

Record each item's primary format and its source work separately. Films and series remain separate categories even when delivered through a video platform. Music denotes standalone listening in the main format comparison. Pictures include standalone images, photographs, illustrations, screenshots, diagrams, advertisements, memes and carousels. Digital books mean reading distributed book text; quotations and adaptations are different exposures. Games may be downloaded or streamed and need not require an always-online connection.

Preserve the original source category in aggregate tables. TV streaming includes films and other programmes as well as series, so it remains a series proxy. Gaming aggregates may mix distribution methods. Source categories cannot be retrospectively partitioned into exclusive format totals, and simultaneous consumption requires elapsed-time deduplication for an attention budget.

### Comparison B: media components across formats

Record identifiable images, text, music and speech both standalone and within other formats. A screenshot displayed inside a video is an image component of a video exposure; an audible soundtrack is a music component. No separate expressive-content test applies. Record identity, visible/audible interval, prominence and uncertainty. Presence, attentive contact and causal contribution are different observations.

The [component inventory](../data/media_components_2026.md) records missing cross-format totals. Reach is the union of people, time the union of eligible intervals, and frequency the union of exposure days within each component. Concurrent components can each have ten seconds of contact without creating twenty seconds of elapsed human attention. View counts alone do not identify component audiences or effects.

## Pictures

Include standalone online images regardless of purpose: personal photographs, illustrations, diagrams, screenshots, advertisements and memes. Record posts, images and carousels separately, with actual visible time and repeated encounters where available. A video slideshow is a video host with image components. Distinguish viewing, creation, editing and redistribution; a repost is not necessarily original creation. Mixed-feed screen time is not pictures-only time.

No matched picture-only reach, person-time, frequency or revenue observation was selected for the common 2026 snapshot or 2021–2025 financial series. These remain NA. The [image-study appraisals](02_literature_review.md#pictures-research-2024-2026) concern narrower settings and outcomes.

## Long-form horizontal online video

The empirical comparison uses the source's broad **long online video** category, including entertainment, news, tutorials, interviews, vlogs, recordings and other purposes. The source does not establish a shared duration cutoff or horizontal orientation. Consequently, aggregate findings concern short versus long online-video source categories; a specifically vertical-versus-horizontal comparison remains unmeasured.

For a future item-level sample, record duration, aspect ratio, genre and source-work type. A proposed landscape/ten-minute subgroup may be tested alongside alternative cutoffs, but does not define the population measured by GWI. Films, series, excerpts and reposts need explicit coding to manage overlap. No exclusion is based on whether the content is expressive or utilitarian. YouTube is a distribution platform rather than a single format. [Platform format guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en).

## Research dimensions

Life influence is studied through two separately reported outcome families: major events and everyday/cumulative changes. These distinguish possible research outcomes, not current radar axes. The radars use six subjective scored axes. The current [author score table](../author-interpretations/subjective_scores_2026.md) contains eight numerical Life impact ratings, one per category, on a 0-10 author scale. Life impact is therefore scored in the author model; it is not an unassessed axis. These judgments are separate from the research outcomes below and are not calibrated estimates of those outcomes.

| Dimension | Preferred measurement | Principal qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | Unique consumers within a fixed week or month | Accounts, memberships, visits, and starts are not unique people |
| Time | Person-hours in a fixed population and period | Concurrent consumption requires de-duplication |
| Frequency | Days used per week | Days are not sessions or content items |
| Money | Revenue under an explicit industry boundary | Platform revenue, trade income, and ticket gross differ |
| Cultural recognition | Recognition of specified works after a stated interval | Catalog age and adaptation complicate comparisons |
| Personal significance | Validated meaningfulness or appreciation measures | A strong experience is not demonstrated life change |
| Everyday affect | Repeated mood level, variability and recovery measures during ordinary use | A repeated state response is not automatically a lasting baseline shift |
| Self-evaluation | Separate repeated state self-esteem/body-evaluation measures from trait measures | Body satisfaction is not a complete measure of self-worth |
| Habits | Repeated observed behavior and validated automaticity measures | Frequent use and intentions alone do not establish a habit |
| Belonging | Repeated connection, loneliness and group-identification measures, kept distinct | Friendship closeness is not identical to cultural belonging |
| Perceived norms | Repeated estimates of what others do and approve, alongside personal endorsement | Descriptive norms, injunctive norms and one's own beliefs are different |
| Belief change | Baseline-to-follow-up change against a comparison group | Direction, magnitude, and durability should be reported |
| Action | Observed, predefined consequential behavior | Intention, clicking, and completed action must remain distinct |
| Persistence | Retained effect at later follow-ups | Retrospective memory is a different outcome |
| Biography | A work's reported role in identity or a turning point | Attribution is subjective and vulnerable to recall bias |

Total person-hours equal population size multiplied by average time across that population. Alternatively, use audience size multiplied by time per audience member. Do not multiply an all-population average by the participation share again. Daily average and annual total imply the same ranking only when denominators and periods match.

## Additional dimensions of mass participation

- **Social breadth:** participation rates within age, education, income or area-deprivation groups. A broad total audience can coexist with unequal participation. Area deprivation must not be substituted for individual income.
- **Territorial breadth:** participation rates within defined regions, using a common questionnaire and population. A country's inclusion in a survey does not by itself show widespread participation there.
- **Media creation:** the population share producing, editing or sharing media over a fixed period, measured separately from consumption.

Social breadth requires format-specific observations with matched definitions and populations; platform adoption is only a proxy.

## Why view counts are unsuitable

Different durations produce different counts for equal time. One hour contains 120 complete 30-second clips, or half of a two-hour film. The resulting 240-to-one count ratio is arithmetic, not evidence that the clips have 240 times the impact.

YouTube changed Shorts views on 31 March 2025 to count starts and replays without a minimum watch duration. Its public statement of over 200 billion daily Shorts views cannot be converted into completed works, unique people, or media influence. [YouTube counting definition](https://developers.google.com/youtube/reporting/revision_history?hl=en); [platform statistics](https://blog.youtube/press/).

## What “life-changing” would require

Life influence includes major events and changes in everyday experience. A biographical turning point is one possible outcome, not the admission criterion for influence. Report the following two families separately, without converting one into the other or an overall score.

| Outcome family | What to record | Required distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major events | Completed move, job change, relationship decision, or another predefined event; occurrence, timing and contribution attributed to the work | Intention and retrospective attribution are not completed action or causal proof |
| Everyday and cumulative changes | Mood and its variability, self-esteem, habits, perceived belonging, and perceived descriptive/injunctive norms; repeated measures of level, trajectory, duration and personal importance | Immediate response, repeated response, baseline shift and post-exposure persistence are different results |

A recurring small change in mood or belonging can matter without a major event. It can be beneficial, harmful, mixed or null; event magnitude does not determine value. Record state self-esteem separately from stable self-evaluation, friendship closeness separately from cultural belonging, and beliefs about what others do/approve separately from personal endorsement. Report unsuccessful regulation and opposing individual trajectories as well as favorable averages.

Use baseline plus repeated diary or experience-sampling measurements to assess everyday influence. Preregister time windows, outcomes and meaningful-change criteria; use comparable validated measures where available. Estimate within-person deviations and longer-term changes separately from differences between people. Repeated short-lived responses do not establish a lasting baseline shift, while failure to find persistence does not erase a recurrent effect during use. Include follow-up after exposure changes or ends when making a persistence claim.

The [cumulative-effects review](08_cumulative_media_effects.md) and [separate results](../results/06_event_and_cumulative_outcomes.md) distinguish direct online evidence, broader platform evidence and out-of-scope mechanisms. For mixed works, record all eligible media components symmetrically, including images, music, text and speech. Presence, attentive contact and a causal contribution are separate observations.

For an event outcome, reach multiplied by the probability of a defined attributable event is one proposed population calculation. It does not measure total life impact or replace everyday trajectories. For everyday outcomes, report population coverage and outcome-specific repeated-measure effects over a stated interval; do not sum correlated states, invent a common unit across scales, or treat the absence of an event as zero influence. Neither calculation is estimated as a cross-media index here.
