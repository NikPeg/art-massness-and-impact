# Additional evidence on short-video consumption growth

Search date: **9 November 2026**. Targeted source search, not a systematic review. This note supplements [growth feasibility](../results/05_growth_feasibility.md); it does not validate the existing cross-snapshot percentage calculations.

## Assessment

Additional evidence supports growth within specific measured populations and services. A new publisher analysis also explicitly reports short video's leading increase among its measured formats. These findings do not independently establish a worldwide growth ranking across all eight project categories, or isolate short vertical video.

## EMARKETER, Starcom and GWI: Global Media Intelligence 2026

The release and author transcript were followed by direct inspection of the user-supplied screenshot of **chart 573622** on 9 November 2026. See the [archived evidence and full transcription](../data/media_growth_h1_2026.md). The screenshot explicitly specifies **H1 2026**, internet users **16–64**, and **percentage change** for 12 categories. Short video is 7:06 weekly and +5.2%; mobile games +4.2%; long video +1.4%; streaming +1.3%; broadcast TV −5.9%. This resolves the former uncertainty over percentage versus absolute ranking and the displayed age range. The author discussion describes year-on-year growth; the chart itself does not print a baseline period. H1 2025 is a contextual interpretation, not a transcribed observation.

Confidence rises from low to **moderate** for the bounded partial H04/H05 proxy findings. Uncertainty intervals, a significance test and matched-wave/country-weight checks remain unavailable. Neither vertical/horizontal orientation nor all eight project categories is isolated. The approximately 21.1-minute increment implied by rounded chart entries agrees with the release's +21 minutes. It is not the older +24-minute cross-release difference. GMI26 and DataReportal share GWI surveys; the chart is not independent replication.

## Ofcom / Ipsos iris: UK adults, repeated audience measurement

[Online Nation 2025, Figure 22, printed p. 35](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/online-research/online-nation/2025/online-nations-report-2025.pdf?v=409837) reports TikTok's monthly UK adult audience at 24.0 million in May 2024 and 27.3 million in May 2025 (+14%, as published). Average daily time per monthly visitor is 27 and 28 minutes respectively. The population is UK internet users aged 18+; smartphones, tablets and computers are covered, TV sets and smart displays excluded. Daily minutes are monthly average visitor time divided by days in the month.

These repeated measures support increased TikTok audience and a small increase in rounded visitor-average time within this measurement system. They do not measure short video across platforms. TikTok includes other activities; the visitor denominator can change. Do not treat 28/27 as a precise population consumption growth rate, or label all-YouTube time as long-form-only. The report flags a YouTube method break before 2024.

## Qustodio: monitored children's app use

[Lost in the Scroll, 2025 report landing page](https://www.qustodio.com/en/lost-in-the-scroll/) reports 10% more daily time on TikTok in 2025 and an average of 2.2 hours. The [publisher's methodology summary](https://www.qustodio.com/en/research/annual-report-2025/) describes anonymized app/web records from over 400,000 families with children aged 4–18 during January–December 2025, alongside separate school data and a parent survey. The indexed full report lists TikTok at 120 minutes in 2024 and 132 in 2025.

This provides behavioral measurement beyond adult survey recall. It describes children in families using monitoring software, not a probability sample of all children worldwide. App time is not verified short-video viewing time. Membership, controls, and user selection can affect comparisons. Treat this as corroboration within the monitored user sample, not a global rate. The full PDF exceeded the retrieval service's size limit; the landing page and methodology summary were read, but detailed denominator rules were not fully audited.

## Further leads requiring verification

- [CNNIC's 57th report](https://www.cnnic.cn/n4/2026/0304/c88-11549.html), released 5 February 2026: the indexed official PDF reports December 2025 short-video total time up 9.43 billion hours (+14.1%) against December 2024, citing QuestMobile. It reports the largest absolute increment among mobile internet segments, not the highest percentage growth. The official PDF repeatedly timed out; classification, monitoring coverage and methods remain unaudited. Retain as a candidate, not an audited growth estimate.
- [Sensor Tower, State of Web 2026](https://sensortower.com/report/state-of-web-2026): an indexed copy of the publisher's report describes US browser Shorts time increasing 55% from October 2024 to March 2026, plus Android-specific Reels measures. The publisher landing page was accessible but the full report's available mirror timed out. Do not promote this to a verified observation until the chart, device coverage, panel and URL-path classification are inspected.

## Comparability checks still required

GWI documents a question redesign from Q4 2024, including recent-day recall, weekly frequency and changed duration bins in [Time spent on media](https://help.globalwebindex.com/en/articles/10725990-time-spent-on-media). Its [Core release history](https://help.globalwebindex.com/en/articles/11679923-gwi-core) records expanded 65+ coverage in Q1 2025, and [June 2026 release notes](https://help.globalwebindex.com/en/articles/15362711-release-notes-june-2026) record Russia's removal from Q1 2026. These changes do not prove every later comparison invalid; they require verification of the exact waves and common analysis universe.

Until that audit, the existing +5.97% and +0.67% remain differences between published values, establishing neither consumption direction nor relative growth. New sources should be reported with their own geography, population, metric and period. Updated qualitative confidence is moderate for the bounded claim that short-video services grew in several measured settings. H04 and H05 now have moderate-confidence partial proxy answers based on the inspected GMI26 chart; a universal fastest-growth conclusion remains unestablished.
