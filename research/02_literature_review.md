# Literature review

This targeted review distinguishes immediate experience, persuasion, and observed action. It does not estimate a universal book-versus-video effect.

## Meaningfulness beyond enjoyment

Oliver and Bartsch (2010), *Appreciation as audience response: Exploring entertainment gratifications beyond hedonism*, distinguish enjoyment from appreciation of moving, meaningful, thought-provoking entertainment. This helps operationalize personal significance. It does not establish that a work caused a job change, a breakup, or migration. [University publication record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/appreciation-as-audience-response-exploring-entertainment-gratifi/).

## Persistence of narrative persuasion

*Long-term Persuasive Effects in Narrative Communication Research: A Meta-Analysis* (2020) compares narrative and non-narrative communication at immediate and delayed measurement. Its abstract reports 14 studies and effects on attitudes, intentions, and delayed behavioral outcomes. Persistence can therefore be measured rather than inferred from duration. Delayed study outcomes should not be equated with lifelong transformation. Access in this review was limited to the indexed abstract; the full-text page could not be retrieved. [Journal of Communication](https://academic.oup.com/joc/article/70/4/473/5880130?login=false).

## Experimental evidence on narrative entertainment

Rahmani, Montano, Groves, and Green (2025), *The persuasive effects of narrative entertainment: a meta-analysis of recent experiments*, synthesizes randomized comparisons. Its principal control comparison includes 63 experimental comparisons in 47 papers. Effects extend beyond beliefs and intentions to message-consistent behavior. The synthesis supports the possibility of causal influence by entertainment; it neither estimates a eight-medium ranking nor measures the probability of a major biographical event after ordinary consumption. [Published article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/persuasive-effects-of-narrative-entertainment-a-metaanalysis-of-recent-experiments/07CABFC5ABA0D3A6A369FCDDB21F8F76).

## Consequential outcomes associated with television stories

La Ferrara, Chong, and Duryea (2012), *Soap Operas and Fertility: Evidence from Brazil*, uses the staggered expansion of Globo coverage. The authors find evidence consistent with soap operas depicting smaller families influencing fertility. This is stronger than a retrospective claim that a favorite story mattered. It is a specific quasi-experimental case with identification assumptions, not evidence that every series changes family decisions or that series outperform books. [Research paper](https://jenni.uchicago.edu/Fertility/Ferrara_Chong_Duryea_2012_AEJAE_v4_n4.pdf).

## Short video and a measured behavioral step

Hu and colleagues (2024), *Using Short-Form Videos to Get Clinical Trial Newcomers to Sign Up: Message-Testing Experiment*, varies message source and framing. The outcome includes clicking a sign-up option for future trials. It does **not** establish actual trial enrollment or completed participation. The study shows why “short video cannot affect behavior” is too strong, while also showing why an initial click cannot stand in for a life-changing decision. Health messaging is not the same as artistic short video. [Full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11362704/).

## Applicability to the added long-video category

No category-specific causal study was added with this extension. Narrative-entertainment findings may motivate hypotheses for narrative essays or authored vlogs, but do not automatically generalize to interviews, instruction, performances, or repeated creator relationships. Neither longer duration nor horizontal orientation establishes stronger persuasion or biographical influence. A new radar is not a new literature result.

## What the literature does not supply

- A common global annual measure of major life decisions attributable to each medium.
- An experimentally established superiority of books over short-form video on the ten radar axes.
- A causal conversion from minutes, revenue, enjoyment, or meaningfulness into changed lives.
- An adequate basis for extrapolating one isolated work's effects to years of consumption of an entire format.

These are discovery leads, not additional fully appraised studies in this dossier. No new effect estimates were calculated from them.
