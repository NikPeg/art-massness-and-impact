# Documented targeted literature search, 9 November 2026

## Status and scope

This is the project's **moderate review upgrade**: a bounded, documented targeted search supporting an exploratory evidence map. It is not a systematic review, a completed rapid review, or an exhaustive census of eight literatures. The log records work actually performed on 9 November 2026; earlier searches were not reconstructed as if they had followed this procedure. Search coverage and confidence in a particular conclusion are separate judgments.

The update asks: (1) what experimental or longitudinal evidence concerns beliefs, observed action, or persistence; (2) whether evidence supports a books-versus-short-video or long-versus-short-video comparison; and (3) whether studies of creator relationships concern the same exposure unit as a single work. Immediate cognition, emotion, attention, and intentions are mapped separately from completed behavior and major life decisions.

## Search execution and stopping rule

Used the available general web-search tool, followed by direct retrieval of publisher pages, institutional repositories, and indexed original abstracts. No direct PsycINFO, Scopus, Web of Science, or systematic PubMed database search was performed. PubMed pages reached through web discovery do not constitute a PubMed database search. Queries were English-language, with no date restriction in the search tool; material was assessed as available by 9 November 2026. Older work was eligible for mechanisms; digital-distribution applicability was assessed separately.

The bounded update consisted of the eight queries below, followed by selected direct retrievals. It stopped after this query set and access checks, not after evidence saturation. There was no comprehensive reference-list or forward-citation search. Web ranking and indexing can change; exact queries make the procedure repeatable, not the returned results identical. No complete export of search hits was retained; counts of all screened records and a PRISMA flow cannot be reconstructed from this log.

## Selection and appraisal rules

Prioritized original experimental/quasi-experimental/longitudinal studies and research syntheses with an explicit method. Kept reviews as syntheses, not independent additional samples alongside their component studies. Included both positive and non-significant findings when retrieved; no effect-direction filter was applied. There was one appraisal pass without independent screening or duplicate extraction.

Retained indirect studies only as context, explicitly identifying mismatches in exposure, artistic content, population, distribution, outcome, or follow-up. Advertising conversion, health messaging, time perception, and viewing prediction cannot directly answer the artistic life-impact question. Commentary, platform advice, recruitment posts, and nonresearch pages were not evidence. Access failure is a retrieval gap, not an exclusion for negative findings. Abstract-only material is provisional; no full risk-of-bias judgment or causal endorsement is based on it.

For each retained source, extracted the available design/exposure, comparator, outcome, follow-up where accessible, and directness. A missing field is unverified, not assumed absent. No pooled estimate, medium league table, or numerical confidence score was calculated.

## Outcome of the update

The bounded search adds experimental and synthesis context, including a non-significant behavioral result; it does not establish comparative superiority of any format. No eligible direct comparison of artistic media on completed major life changes was selected and appraised in this update. This means **not established within this search**, not that no such study exists. Long-video-specific causal coverage remains incomplete, and creator-relationship evidence cannot fill that gap automatically.

The [literature review](../research/02_literature_review.md#documented-targeted-update-9-november-2026) interprets these sources. The [further-work plan](../FURTHER_WORK.md) specifies the work needed before calling the project a full systematic review.
