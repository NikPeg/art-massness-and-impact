# Major events and everyday or cumulative changes: separate results

Life influence is reported in two equally relevant outcome families: major events and everyday/cumulative changes. Neither substitutes for the other. The [study-by-study appraisal](../research/08_cumulative_media_effects.md) records designs, findings, confidence, retrieval limits and original sources.

## Major events

The evidence still does not supply comparable attributable rates of completed moves, job changes or relationship decisions across the eight media. Existing bounded narrative/behavioral cases remain in the [literature review](../research/02_literature_review.md); they do not establish a universal ranking. This lack of event-level evidence says nothing by itself about the presence or absence of everyday influence.

## Everyday and cumulative changes

Image-exposure evidence is assigned to **pictures**, one of the project's eight categories. Instagram identifies where the images were delivered, not the scope of the outcome claim. Mixed-platform studies remain separate evidence that cannot identify which media-content category contributed. See the [evidence-to-category mapping](../research/08_cumulative_media_effects.md#which-project-categories-the-evidence-concerns).

These results justify broadening the operational definition of influence. They do not show that all brief encounters accumulate, that every change persists, or that one medium has more total life impact. A repeated benefit or harm during exposure and a lasting change after exposure are both relevant but different findings. Lack of a major event cannot be coded as zero everyday impact.

The [study proposal](03_next_study.md#outcomes) specifies separate event and everyday/cumulative outputs, baseline and repeated measures, and follow-up persistence. The current [radar score table](../author-interpretations/subjective_scores_2026.md) contains eight numerical Life impact ratings, one per category. They are author judgments on a 0-10 scale, not missing or unassessed values. This heterogeneous literature supplies no calibrated numerical comparison and does not validate those existing scores.

## Relation to the hypothesis statuses

The outcome map does not establish H12: the category-specific causal effect and post-exposure persistence of a short-video feed are not tested by image and mixed-platform results. Their bounded findings retain their own confidence, while H12 remains unanswered under the [common scope-fit rule](../research/05_methods_and_reproducibility.md#hypothesis-answer-status-and-scope-fit). Offline norm-change examples remain methodological context.

## Format and component attribution

Report outcomes by primary work format separately from component-level questions. The [component inventory](../data/media_components_2026.md) includes images shown inside a vertical video on the same basis as an embedded soundtrack. A video-format effect is not automatically an effect caused by its image or musical component. Current studies remain classified by the actual exposure and comparator they tested; no new component effect size or audience total is inferred from this scope correction.
